# Evidence and design limits

Primary sources checked on 2026-11-15. These sources support specific design decisions; none validates this complete assessment, its passage counts, or a universal personal learning-style score. The original conversation's numerical research summaries were not full citations. Do not reuse their effect sizes, especially recent or incompletely identified claims, without independent verification.

## Basis for the protocol

- **Preference matching:** Rogowsky, Calhoun, and Tallal (2015), *Matching Learning Style to Instructional Method: Effects on Comprehension*. In college-educated adults randomized to audiobook or e-text, measured preferences did not reliably interact with mode for immediate or two-week comprehension. This limits that matching claim; it does not establish that every text format is equally effective for every reader. Keep preferences separate from performance. [Primary article](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/features/edu-a0037478.pdf).

- **Individual experimental comparisons:** Shamseer et al. (2015), *CONSORT extension for reporting N-of-1 trials (CENT) 2015: Explanation and elaboration*. Items 8a–c address sequence allocation; item 12c addresses carryover, period effects, and dependence within a participant. This is guidance for N-of-1 clinical trials. Applying its design principles to text comparisons is an explicit methodological adaptation, not validation of an educational test. Use repeated comparisons and report realized order and interruptions. [Primary guidance and explanation](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.h1793).

- **Cohesion and prior knowledge:** McNamara, Kintsch, Songer, and Kintsch (1996), *Are Good Texts Always Better? Interactions of Text Coherence, Background Knowledge, and Levels of Understanding in Learning From Text*. Biology-text experiments found that coherence and prior knowledge interacted differently across measures; generating links sometimes benefited knowledgeable readers on deeper tasks. Measure prior knowledge and more than literal recall. Do not prescribe low coherence for all experts. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1401_1).

- **Reading skill and task dependence:** Ozuru, Dempsey, and McNamara (2009), *Prior knowledge, reading skill, and text cohesion in the comprehension of science texts*. The study used counterbalanced biology passages, distinct question types, and prewritten scoring. Higher cohesion supported text-based comprehension; interactions did not reduce to a universal expertise-reversal rule. This supports separating factual, bridging-inference, and broader conceptual outcomes. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2008.04.003).

- **Testing changes retention:** Roediger and Karpicke (2006), *Test-Enhanced Learning: Taking Memory Tests Improves Long-Term Retention*. Prose experiments compared restudy with free recall without feedback. Restudy favored short-delay recall; retrieval favored later retention. Immediate assessment is itself a learning intervention. Standardize it across conditions and record feedback and previous tests delivered by the assessment. [Primary article](https://psychology.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/216/2019/03/Roediger-Karpicke-2006.pdf).

- **Inference as an outcome:** Karpicke and Blunt (2011), *Retrieval Practice Produces More Learning than Elaborative Studying with Concept Mapping*. Science-text experiments assessed directly stated information and inference after one week. This illustrates why factual and inferential outcomes deserve separate measurement; it does not establish that one format is optimal for this user. [Primary article](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf).

- **Perceived versus measured learning:** Deslauriers et al. (2019), *Measuring actual learning versus feeling of learning in response to being actively engaged in the classroom*. A randomized physics-class comparison found higher measured learning with active instruction despite lower perceived learning. This illustrates the distinction between feeling and outcome; it is not a comparison of typography or prose styles. [Primary article](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1821936116).

## What the skill adds

Autonomous setup, short comparison blocks, balanced passage pairs, the copy/paste result packet, timing rules, and confidence labels are practical design choices. An initial block of two matched pairs targeting roughly 8–10 minutes is a workload example, not a power calculation or a validated minimum for a complete profile. Select further comparisons by their likely value rather than a fixed long battery. Timing visible content estimates exposure, not attention, reading fluency, or neural processing.

Language, tone, examples, structure, and display are candidate factors to test, not pre-ranked treatments. Match content and assessment quality, acknowledge remaining topic differences, and confirm on unseen material. Font preference and accessibility adjustments can be useful without proving a retention benefit. A comprehensive profile can be scientifically honest only if it explicitly records what remains unknown.
